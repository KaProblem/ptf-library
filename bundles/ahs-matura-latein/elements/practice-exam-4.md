# Introduction

- Try it under exam conditions: 270 minutes, a dictionary, no other help. Start with the translation text, then do the interpretation tasks.
- This paper follows the structure of the 4-year Latin Matura: a translation text (36 points) and an interpretation text with ten tasks (24 points). The texts and tasks are written for this course.
- Afterwards the learner knows how long each part takes and where they still lose points.

# Core

A Matura paper is solved in two parts. The translation text is marked
with 12 sense units, 6 vocabulary, 6 morphology and 6 syntax checkpoints
and up to 6 language points; the interpretation part has ten tasks from
simple (loanwords, word formation, sentence analysis) to open (comparison,
position, creative text). Both parts need at least half of their points.
The learner should first read both texts and all tasks, then translate,
then solve the interpretation tasks in an order that secures the easy
points first.

# Key facts

- Points: translation 36 (minimum 18), interpretation 24 (minimum 12); bands 60–53, 52–45, 44–37, 36–30, 29–0.
- Suggested time: 10 min reading, 120 min translation (incl. revision), 110 min interpretation, 30 min final check.
- Translation text below: 111 words; interpretation text: about 87 words.

# Examples

**A. Translation text** (written for this course after a story told by
Pliny the Younger)

Introduction: In the Roman town of Hippo on the coast of North Africa,
boys used to compete in swimming out into the sea.

> 1 In colonia Hipponensi¹ pueri cotidie in mari natabant²; qui longissime
> 2 a litore processerat, victor habebatur. Quodam die puer quidam audacior
> 3 ceteris longius natavit. Subito delphinus³ ei occurrit et nunc ante eum,
> 4 nunc post eum natabat. Deinde puerum timentem dorso⁴ sustulit et primo in
> 5 altum⁵ portavit, mox ad litus reduxit. Haec fama per totam urbem celeriter
> 6 divulgata⁶ est. Postero die homines ad litus concurrerunt, ut ipsi
> 7 spectaculum viderent. Delphinus rursus apparuit et puerum, qui iam non
> 8 timebat, iterum vexit⁷. Cum multi cives eum blande vocarent, animal etiam
> 9 tangi se patiebatur. Sed magistratus, quod tot homines ex agris venerant et
> 10 urbem sumptibus⁸ gravabant⁸, delphinum occidi iusserunt. Ita animal mite⁹,
> 11 quod pueros amaverat, periit.

¹ Hipponensis, -e: of Hippo · ² natare: swim · ³ delphinus, -i m.: dolphin ·
⁴ dorsum, -i n.: back · ⁵ altum, -i n.: the open sea · ⁶ divulgare: spread ·
⁷ vehere, veho, vexi: carry · ⁸ sumptibus gravare: burden with costs ·
⁹ mitis, -e: gentle

Marking key (expected content and checkpoints):

| SE | Passage | Expected content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | In colonia … natabant | Boys in Hippo swim in the sea every day. |
| 2 | qui longissime … habebatur | Whoever has swum farthest out counts as the winner. |
| 3 | Quodam die … natavit | One day a bolder boy swims farther than the others. |
| 4 | Subito … natabat | A dolphin meets him and swims around him. |
| 5 | Deinde … reduxit | It carries the frightened boy on its back out to sea and back to the shore. |
| 6 | Haec fama … divulgata est | The news spreads quickly through the town. |
| 7 | Postero die … viderent | Next day people hurry to the shore to see it themselves. |
| 8 | Delphinus rursus … vexit | The dolphin comes again and carries the boy, who is no longer afraid. |
| 9 | Cum multi … patiebatur | The animal even lets the people touch it. |
| 10 | Sed magistratus, quod … gravabant | The many visitors from the countryside cost the town a lot. |
| 11 | delphinum occidi iusserunt | The officials order the dolphin to be killed. |
| 12 | Ita animal … periit | So the gentle animal that had loved the boys dies. |

LE: habebatur (was considered, not: had) · occurrit (came to meet, not:
happened) · sustulit (lifted up, not: removed, endured) · altum (open sea,
not: height) · blande (coaxingly, kindly) · patiebatur (allowed, not:
suffered).
MO: processerat (pluperfect) · audacior (comparative, agreeing with puer)
· longius (comparative adverb) · timentem (PPA, acc. sg., agreeing with
puerum) · divulgata est (perfect passive, agreeing with fama) ·
magistratus (nom. pl., subject of iusserunt).
SY: ceteris (ablative of comparison) · puerum timentem (Pc) · ut …
viderent (GS final) · cum … vocarent (GS temporal/causal, simultaneous) ·
tangi se (AcI, simultaneous) · quod … gravabant (GS causal).

**B. Interpretation text** (adapted from Seneca, Epistulae morales 47)

Introduction: The philosopher Seneca writes to his friend Lucilius, who
treats his slaves in an unusual way.

> 1 Libenter ex iis, qui a te veniunt, cognovi familiariter¹ te cum servis
> 2 tuis vivere. Hoc prudentiam tuam, hoc eruditionem decet. „Servi sunt."
> 3 Immo² homines. „Servi sunt." Immo contubernales³. „Servi sunt." Immo
> 4 humiles amici. „Servi sunt." Immo conservi, si cogitaveris tantundem⁴ in
> 5 utrosque licere⁴ fortunae. Cogita istum, quem servum tuum vocas, ex isdem
> 6 seminibus⁵ ortum eodem frui caelo, aeque spirare, aeque vivere, aeque
> 7 mori! Ostende, quis non sit servus: alius libidini servit, alius
> 8 avaritiae, alius ambitioni, omnes timori. Sic cum inferiore vivas,
> 9 quemadmodum tecum superiorem velis vivere.

¹ familiaris, -e: friendly, as with one's family · ² immo: no, rather ·
³ contubernalis, -is m.: housemate, comrade · ⁴ tantundem licere (+ dat.):
to have just as much power · ⁵ semen, -inis n.: seed, origin

Comparison text for task 7 (Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948,
Article 1): "All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and
rights. They are endowed with reason and conscience and should act
towards one another in a spirit of brotherhood."

# Misconceptions

- **translate-everything-first**: Polish the translation until the end and then rush the interpretation → the IT needs at least 12 points of its own; plan the time for both parts.
- **ignore-notes**: Glosses and introductions are optional → they carry content needed for sense units and tasks.

# Tutor notes

- Let the learner do the paper without help and under time; mark it afterwards with the key, checkpoint by checkpoint, and discuss the lost points.
- Do not reveal the marking key or the sample answers before the learner has finished.
- The texts are written for this course; real past papers (published by the BMBWF) are better for the final weeks. When the learner brings one, mark it together in the same way.
