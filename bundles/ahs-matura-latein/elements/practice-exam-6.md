# Introduction

- Try it under exam conditions: 270 minutes, a dictionary, no other help.
- This paper follows the structure of the 6-year Latin Matura: the same 36 + 24 points as the 4-year paper, but the texts use more constructions per sentence (gerundive, future infinitive, relative connection, predicative). The texts and tasks are written for this course.
- Afterwards the learner knows how a full paper feels and which checkpoint types still cost points.

# Core

The 6-year paper has the same structure and marking as the 4-year
paper: 12 sense units, 6 vocabulary, 6 morphology and 6 syntax
checkpoints and up to 6 language points for the translation, ten tasks
for 24 points on the interpretation text, at least half of the points in
each part. Learners of 6-year Latin are expected to handle longer and
more complex sentences; the method stays the same: read everything,
decode the predicates and constructions, translate by sense units, and
secure the easy interpretation points first.

# Key facts

- Points: translation 36 (minimum 18), interpretation 24 (minimum 12); bands 60–53, 52–45, 44–37, 36–30, 29–0.
- Translation text below: 109 words; interpretation text: 83 words.
- Constructions in the translation text: result clause, relative clause of purpose, future infinitive in an AcI, concessive clause, predicative gerundive, relative connection with a deponent participle, request clause, predicative.

# Examples

**A. Translation text** (written for this course after a story told by
Cicero and other ancient authors)

Introduction: Dionysius was tyrant of Syracuse in Sicily. Damon and
Phintias were followers of the philosopher Pythagoras.

> 1 Damon et Phintias, Pythagorae discipuli, tanta amicitia inter se coniuncti
> 2 erant, ut alter pro altero mori paratus esset. Cum Dionysius tyrannus
> 3 Phintiam capitis¹ damnavisset¹, hic tempus petivit, quo domum proficisci et
> 4 res suas ordinare posset. Tyrannus se eum dimissurum esse negavit, nisi
> 5 aliquis pro eo vas² fieret. Statim Damon se vadem² obtulit, quamquam sciebat
> 6 se ipsum moriturum esse, si amicus non rediret. Iam dies supremus aderat et
> 7 Phintias nondum redierat. Omnes Damonem stultitiae accusabant; ille tamen
> 8 de fide amici numquam dubitavit. Hora ipsa, qua Damon necandus erat,
> 9 Phintias advenit. Nam tempestas iter eius retardaverat³. Quorum fidem
> 10 admiratus Dionysius supplicium remisit et eos rogavit, ut se tertium in
> 11 amicitiam reciperent.

¹ capitis damnare: sentence to death · ² vas, vadis m.: guarantor, surety ·
³ retardare: delay

Marking key (expected content and checkpoints):

| SE | Passage | Expected content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Damon et Phintias … esset | Two followers of Pythagoras are such close friends that each would die for the other. |
| 2 | Cum Dionysius … damnavisset | The tyrant has sentenced Phintias to death. |
| 3 | hic tempus petivit … posset | Phintias asks for time to go home and settle his affairs. |
| 4 | Tyrannus … fieret | The tyrant will only let him go if someone stands surety for him. |
| 5 | Statim Damon se vadem obtulit | Damon at once offers himself as surety. |
| 6 | quamquam … rediret | He knows that he himself will die if his friend does not return. |
| 7 | Iam dies supremus … redierat | The last day has come and Phintias is not back yet. |
| 8 | Omnes … accusabant | Everyone calls Damon a fool. |
| 9 | ille tamen … dubitavit | Damon never doubts his friend's loyalty. |
| 10 | Hora ipsa … advenit | Phintias arrives at the very hour of the execution. |
| 11 | Nam tempestas … retardaverat | A storm had delayed him. |
| 12 | Quorum fidem … reciperent | Dionysius admires their loyalty, cancels the punishment and asks to join their friendship as a third. |

LE: coniuncti (bound, united) · petivit (asked for, not: attacked) ·
ordinare (settle, arrange) · supremus (last, not: highest) · accusabant
(reproached, accused of) · remisit (cancelled, waived, not: sent back).
MO: damnavisset (pluperfect subjunctive) · posset (imperfect subjunctive)
· dimissurum esse (future infinitive) · aderat (imperfect of adesse) ·
necandus (gerundive, agreeing with Damon) · admiratus (deponent perfect
participle, agreeing with Dionysius).
SY: ut … esset (GS consecutive) · quo … posset (relative clause of
purpose) · se eum dimissurum esse (AcI, subsequent) · quamquam … sciebat
(GS concessive) · necandus erat (predicative gerundive) · Quorum
(relative connection).

**B. Interpretation text** (written for this course after the story in
Aulus Gellius)

Introduction: Years after he had helped a wounded lion in Africa, the
runaway slave Androclus was caught and thrown to wild animals in the
Circus in Rome. Among them was a huge lion.

> 1 Leo, simul ac Androclum procul vidit, subito quasi admirans stetit ac
> 2 deinde sensim¹ et placide ad hominem accessit. Tum caudam more adulantium²
> 3 canum movens corpori hominis se adiunxit et crura manusque eius lingua
> 4 leniter permulsit³. Androclus, qui prae metu paene exanimatus erat, animum
> 5 recepit et leonem agnovit. Homo et leo inter se laeti salutaverunt.
> 6 Populus, qui hoc spectaculum mirum viderat, magna voce clamavit. Caesar
> 7 Androclum ad se vocavit et causam rogavit. Ille rem totam narravit.
> 8 Itaque Androclus liberatus est et leo ei donatus.

¹ sensim: slowly · ² adulari: fawn, wag the tail · ³ permulcere, -mulsi:
stroke, lick

Comparison text for task 8 (written for this course): "In a wildlife
reserve, a lioness that had been raised by a ranger was released into the
wild. Three years later the ranger visited the reserve. The lioness came
running out of the bush, rubbed her head against him and purred like a
house cat."

# Misconceptions

- **translate-everything-first**: Polish the translation until the end and then rush the interpretation → the IT needs at least 12 points of its own; plan the time for both parts.
- **dense-sentences**: Long 6-year sentences are translated word by word → decode predicates and constructions first; split long sentences in German.

# Tutor notes

- Let the learner do the paper without help and under time; mark it afterwards with the key, checkpoint by checkpoint.
- Do not reveal the marking key or the sample answers before the learner has finished.
- The texts are written for this course; real past papers (published by the BMBWF) are better for the final weeks. When the learner brings one, mark it together in the same way.
