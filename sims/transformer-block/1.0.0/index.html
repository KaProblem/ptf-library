How GPT works · The block

Listen, then think

A transformer is made of many identical building blocks. Each block has two parts: first the words exchange information (several attention heads at once), then each word thinks it over on its own (feed-forward). Here you take both parts apart.

Station 1 · Several heads

Three heads, three questions

A single attention head can only ask one kind of question. So several heads work in parallel, each with its own query, key and value numbers. Below you see whom each word (row) listens to (column) in each head. Dark means a lot of attention. Sentence: "The cat drinks milk because she is thirsty".

Question: Which head almost always looks at the word directly before?

The outputs of the heads are concatenated and projected back to the model width with a matrix. Example: 4 heads of 8 dimensions give 32 again. More heads do not cost more width; they split it.

Station 2 · Each on its own

Feed-forward: thinking alone

After listening, each word has gathered new information. Now each word sends its vector through the same small network, but each word separately. Change the feature of "cat" and watch which rows change.

Formula per word: FF(x) = ReLU(x·W1 + b1)·W2 + b2. The inner layer is usually 4 times as wide as the model (here 3 → 12 → 3). About two thirds of a block's parameters sit in these layers.
