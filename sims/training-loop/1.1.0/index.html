How GPT works · Training

Learning by nudging

The smallest language model there is: a table of numbers. Each row belongs to one character and holds a score for every character that could come next. At first the table knows nothing. Training changes its numbers a tiny bit at a time until the predictions fit the text. Here the text is a single line: "a banana and a bandana".

Station 1 · The model is a table

Look up a row

The text uses five characters (␣ is the space). Tap a character: the model looks up its row of scores, and softmax turns the scores into probabilities for the next character. The table has not been trained yet.

Guess first: In the text, "b" is always followed by "a". What probability does the untrained table give "a" after "b"?

In code this table is nn.Embedding(vocab_size, vocab_size): looking up row i is the same as multiplying a one-hot vector by the table.

Station 2 · One training step

Nudge the numbers

One example from the text: after "" comes "". The loss says how surprised the model is: −ln(probability of the right answer). One training step raises the score of the right character a little and lowers the others. Press the button a few times and watch the probability of the right answer.

Probability of the right answer
Loss for this example

The direction comes from the gradient of the loss. For softmax with cross-entropy it is simple: score j changes by −step size · (pj − 1) for the right character and −step size · pj for every other one. Wrong characters that the model believed in most are pushed down hardest.

Station 3 · Many steps

The training loop

Now all 23 examples of the text at once: compute the average loss, nudge every row, repeat. Choose a step size (learning rate) and train. The dashed line is the best any bigram table can reach on this text.

Guess first: You will try step sizes 0.1, 5 and 40. Which one reaches a low loss fastest?

for step in range(max_iters):
    xb, yb = get_batch("train")
    logits, loss = model(xb, yb)        # 1. how wrong are we?
    optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
    loss.backward()                     # 2. which direction lowers the loss?
    optimizer.step()                    # 3. move every number a little
Station 4 · Writing

Generate, one character at a time

To write, the model looks up the row of the last character, rolls a die weighted with the probabilities, appends the result and starts again. Compare the untrained table with the one you trained in station 3.
