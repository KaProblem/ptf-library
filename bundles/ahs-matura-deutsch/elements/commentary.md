# Introduction

- Try it: a small town wants to close its outdoor pool because a renovation would cost 2.4 million euros. Write a headline of at most six words that already shows your opinion.
- A Kommentar is a published opinion on a debated public issue. Readers know the news; they want a clear, well-argued judgement in a few paragraphs.
- Afterwards the learner can write a Kommentar with a clear position, fair weighing and a pointed ending.

# Core

A Kommentar judges an issue that is debated in public, written for a
medium with some reach. The writer can be a journalist or, as is usual in
the Matura, someone the issue concerns (a student, a resident) in the role
the task names. It presents the occasion briefly so that readers know what it
is about, weighs the most important arguments (including the other side's)
and reaches a clear judgement, often with a demand. A pointed headline, a
concrete opening and a memorable last sentence are typical; irony,
rhetorical questions, ellipses and short sentences are welcome if they
serve the argument, and the text stays impersonal (usually no „ich“). Unlike a Leserbrief it does not answer one particular article,
and unlike an Erörterung it does not have to treat both sides evenly: it
is openly opinionated.

# Key facts

- Structure: headline (pointed, often a judgement or wordplay); introduction with the problem as the Textbeilage presents it, in two or three sentences; main part with the argument in one of three patterns (thesis, arguments with evidence or limits, conclusion; or question, weighing, answer; or problem, weighing, attempted solution), with arguments that stand out one by one but are linked (contrast, support, restriction); conclusion with a clear position, often a demand or pointed final sentence; author's name.
- Role and readers: written for publication (newspaper, school newspaper, website). The writer may be a journalist or, as the Matura usually sets it, a student, resident or other person concerned, in the role the task names. Readers are an interested public.
- Impersonal style: as a rule no „ich“; say „Man muss …“, „Wer … , sollte …“ instead.
- Own thought: go beyond the Textbeilage by adding, extending or refuting its arguments; the account of the facts is short and serves the judgement.
- Judgement: clear, recognisable early, justified; fairness to the other side makes it stronger.
- Means: compressed content and arguments sharpened to a point; rhetorical question, ellipsis, short sentence, irony, contrast, a pointed final sentence (Pointe).
- Kommentar vs Leserbrief: the Leserbrief is a reader's letter that answers a specific article, with address and closing formula; the Kommentar is a published opinion piece with more complex argument, impersonal and without letter form.
- Kommentar vs Erörterung: they argue alike, but the Erörterung is a school text type without a situation that weighs all sides; the Kommentar is written for a medium, presents its arguments more compactly and is openly pointed.
- Length in the exam: 270–330, 405–495 or 540–660 words; the task always names a situation (medium and role). Too long a Kommentar loses the compression it needs.

# Level: highschool

<!-- ptf:text v1 -->

A good headline from the try-it task might be „Ein Bad ist mehr als ein
Budgetposten“ or „Sparen am falschen Becken“: it states a judgement before
the first sentence. That is the spirit of a Kommentar: an opinion, clearly
argued.

Start with the occasion in two or three sentences: what happened, who
decided, why. Readers may know the news, but the Kommentar must be
understandable on its own.

Then weigh. Give the other side its due („Man muss dem Bürgermeister
zugutehalten, dass er rechnet.“), then show why your view weighs more. Use
concrete details from the facts: 900 visitors a day, 25 kilometres to the
next pool. A rhetorical question or a comparison can sharpen a point.

End with a judgement or demand and a last sentence that sticks, the
Pointe: „Sparen ist richtig. Aber dort, wo Kinder schwimmen lernen, ist es
die teuerste Form des Sparens.“

# Misconceptions

- **neutral-comment**: A Kommentar presents both sides neutrally and leaves the decision to the reader → it takes a clear position; fairness to the other side serves the judgement.
- **opinion-without-facts**: A Kommentar is pure opinion and needs no facts → the occasion is presented accurately, and the judgement rests on facts and arguments.
- **comment-is-letter**: A Kommentar is a Leserbrief with a headline → the Leserbrief answers one article in letter form; the Kommentar is an impersonal opinion piece for a medium with fuller argument.
- **only-journalists**: Only a journalist can write a Kommentar → in the Matura the writer is usually a student or citizen concerned by the issue; what makes it a Kommentar is publication, a clear thesis and the argument.
- **pointe-is-joke**: The Pointe is a joke at the end → it is a sharp final sentence that sums up the judgement.

# Tutor notes

- The learner may argue for closing the pool; judge the quality of the weighing and the judgement, not the side.
- Have the learner write the headline and the last sentence first; they force a clear position.
- The Textbeilage and its newspaper are invented for this course; tell the learner if they ask.

## Blueprint for new tasks

Rules for all tasks: exam-format element, "Making new writing tasks".

- **Word range:** 270–330, 405–495 or 540–660.
- **Situation (required):** a newspaper, school newspaper or news website and a role: a journalist, or, as usual in the Matura, a student, resident or other person concerned by the issue („als Schülervertreterin für die Schülerzeitung“).
- **Textbeilage:** a news report on a current, debatable decision or development, or a graphic with figures (survey, statistics) plus a short text; 200–600 words. Both sides should be visible in it.
- **Operators:** bullet 1 „Stellen Sie … kurz dar“ / „Beschreiben Sie …“ / „Fassen Sie die Ergebnisse … zusammen“; bullet 2 „Bewerten Sie …“ / „Beurteilen Sie …“ / „Erläutern Sie …“; bullet 3 „Nehmen Sie Stellung zu …“ / „Machen Sie einen Vorschlag …“ / „Kommentieren Sie …“.
- **Rubric template:** follow the rubric of the task above. Inhalt: writing acts of a Kommentar; one line per bullet; Textbeilage grasped and facts correct, none invented; own thought beyond the Textbeilage. Textstruktur: headline, problem, argument pattern, clear position at the end, source named. Stil: suited to medium and readers, impersonal, pointed, own formulations. Sprachnormen. Range (too long hurts here).
