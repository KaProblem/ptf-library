# Introduction

- Try it: here is the first line of a writing task, "Verfassen Sie einen Leserbrief (270–330 Wörter)". Before reading on, guess: how much text is that on a handwritten page, and how long would you need for it?
- The German Matura is a writing exam. There is no list of topics to learn by heart; what counts is reading a task exactly and writing the text type it asks for.
- Afterwards the learner knows the format, can take any writing task apart into what it demands and can plan the five hours.

# Core

In the written German Matura the candidate chooses one of three theme
packages (Themenpakete) and writes both of its two writing tasks
(Schreibaufträge), together about 900 words. Each task names a text type,
a situation, one or more source texts (Textbeilagen), a word range and
usually three work instructions (Arbeitsaufträge) that each begin with an
operator such as "fassen Sie zusammen" or "nehmen Sie Stellung". The text
is assessed by how fully it carries out exactly these instructions in the
named text type, so reading the task carefully is the first skill.

# Key facts

- Duration: 300 minutes (five hours) for both texts, including reading and choosing.
- Three theme packages with two independent writing tasks each; the candidate chooses one package and writes both of its tasks. A package has a common theme, but the two tasks have their own source texts.
- Length: each task states its own word range, set at a target ± 10 %: 270–330 (300), 405–495 (450) or 540–660 (600) words. The two texts of a package add up to about 900 words: 270–330 + 540–660, or 405–495 + 405–495.
- The seven text types (since May 2020): Erörterung, Kommentar, Leserbrief, Meinungsrede, Textanalyse, Textinterpretation, Zusammenfassung. Empfehlung and Offener Brief were dropped.
- Every task has at least one Textbeilage, linear (text) or non-linear (graphic, statistics, chart); together at most about 2,000 words per package. One of the six tasks is a Textinterpretation of a literary text (prose, poem or drama scene). There is no reading list: no knowledge of particular works is expected.
- Parts of a Schreibauftrag: theme, text type and word range, situation (where the text appears, who reads it), Textbeilage(n) with source, and the Aufgabenstellung with usually three bullet points.
- Operators by demand (Anforderungsbereiche) in the official catalogue, an open list: I reproduction (nennen, beschreiben, wiedergeben, zusammenfassen); II reorganisation and transfer (analysieren/untersuchen, bestimmen/einordnen/zuordnen, charakterisieren, erklären, erläutern, erschließen, in Beziehung setzen, vergleichen/gegenüberstellen); III reflection and problem solving (appellieren, begründen, beurteilen, bewerten, deuten/interpretieren, diskutieren/erörtern/sich auseinandersetzen mit, entwerfen, kommentieren/Stellung nehmen, überprüfen, vorschlagen). The areas are not strictly separable: describing a Textbeilage is I, describing one's own ideas is III, and a higher area is not always harder.
- Close pairs: beurteilen reaches an own judgement with arguments; bewerten does the same and also makes the own standards of value explicit. Erklären traces something back to causes; erläutern makes it clear with extra information or examples. Erschließen draws out what the text does not say explicitly.
- Each package as a whole demands all three areas. A task has three, at most four, Arbeitsaufträge, each with one operator; operators of area III come after ones of area I or II.
- A text that leaves out a bullet point, or writes another text type, misses part of the task. In the dimension Inhalt this pulls the level down, and ignoring the instructions or missing the central writing act makes it "nicht erfüllt", however good the language is.
- A useful time plan: about 15 minutes to read all six tasks and choose, about 15 minutes at the end to proofread, the rest split in proportion to the word ranges. With 300 + 600 words that is 90 and 180 minutes.
- Count your own handwriting once: words on ten lines, divided by ten, gives your words per line; then you know how many lines a range means instead of counting words in the exam.

# Level: highschool

<!-- ptf:text v1 -->

Start with the task line from the introduction: a Leserbrief of 270 to 330
words. That is a range around 300 words, plus or minus ten percent. Every
writing task in the German Matura has such a range, and the two tasks of
one package add up to about 900 words.

On exam day there are three packages, each with a theme such as "Arbeit
und Freizeit" or "Natur". Read all six tasks first and choose the package
where you can do both tasks well, not the one with the most interesting
theme. You then write both texts of that package.

Every task has the same parts: the text type with the word range, a short
situation that says where the text will appear and who reads it, one or
two source texts, and the instructions. The instructions are the heart of
the task. Each bullet point starts with a verb, the operator, and each
operator asks for a different act of writing. "Geben Sie wieder" means
reproduce the content neutrally; "Analysieren Sie" means show how a text
works; "Nehmen Sie Stellung" means give and justify your own opinion.
Mark the three operators before you write and plan one part of your text
for each.

Time: five hours for about 900 words. Take a quarter of an hour to read
and choose, a quarter of an hour at the end to proofread, and split the
rest in proportion to the lengths.

# Misconceptions

- **theme-over-task**: Choose the package with the most interesting theme → choose the package where both text types and all instructions suit you; the theme alone earns no points.
- **more-is-better**: Writing more words than the range shows effort → the range is part of the task; a text far outside it misses the task, and every extra sentence is another chance for mistakes.
- **free-essay**: The Matura is a free essay on a topic → it asks for one named text type with given instructions; content that answers none of the bullet points earns nothing.
- **operators-interchangeable**: "Beschreiben", "analysieren" and "bewerten" all mean "write something about it" → each operator demands its own writing act; describing where a judgement is asked leaves the bullet point unanswered.
- **one-text-only**: You choose one of the two tasks in a package → you write both tasks of the chosen package.

# Tutor notes

- Use this element first and briefly; come back to it whenever the learner starts a writing task.
- Let the learner mark the operators in every task before writing, throughout the course.
- The exact current regulations (allowed dictionary, dates) are announced by the school; when the learner asks about them, tell them to check with their teacher.
- When the learner brings a past Matura task (photo or PDF), take it apart with them as in `take-task-apart` instead of writing it for them.

## Making new writing tasks

When the learner has done the tasks of this bundle, write new ones in the
exam format. Each text-type element has a blueprint (operators, situations,
source texts, rubric) in its tutor notes; these rules apply to all of them.

- **Parts, in this order:** Thema (two to four words); text type with word range in brackets; situation (medium, readers or audience, occasion: „für die Schülerzeitung“, „vor dem Schulforum“); the Textbeilage(n) with author, title, medium and date; „Aufgabenstellung:“ with three bullet points, each starting with an operator. Write the task in German, in the „Sie“ form of the exam.
- **Three (at most four) bullet points from rising demand:** the first usually reproduces (I: wiedergeben, beschreiben, zusammenfassen), the second works with the material (II: erläutern, erklären, analysieren, untersuchen, vergleichen, erschließen), the third judges or acts (III: Stellung nehmen, bewerten, beurteilen, erörtern, sich auseinandersetzen, appellieren, deuten, vorschlagen). An operator of area III never stands first. Prefer the operator combinations the element's blueprint lists, so the task stays within what the exam asks.
- **One operator per bullet, no questions:** not „Analysieren Sie … und nehmen Sie Stellung“ (two acts in one bullet), and not „Was halten Sie von …?“ (a question suggests or anticipates the answer).
- **Word range:** 270–330, 405–495 or 540–660, as the blueprint says for the text type.
- **Textbeilage:** prefer a real, current text: a newspaper or magazine article, commentary, report, interview, speech or graphic the learner finds or brings, or that you can open. Give its exact source. For literature use a text the learner has in front of them (school reader, Projekt Gutenberg, Wikisource); never write a literary text from memory and attribute it to an author. If no real text is at hand, write your own non-fiction text and say that it is invented. Length: about one page (400–900 words) for analysis, summary and Erörterung; a shorter text or a graphic with figures is enough for Leserbrief, Kommentar and Meinungsrede; at most about 2,000 words for both tasks of a package. Everything a bullet asks about must be in the Textbeilage in enough detail to write about.
- **Theme package (Themenpaket) for a mock exam:** one theme, two independent tasks with different text types and their own Textbeilagen, one 270–330 + one 540–660 (or 405–495 + 405–495), about 900 words in all. For a full mock offer three packages; one of the six tasks is a Textinterpretation. 300 minutes.
- **Rubric before the learner writes:** follow the criteria of the grid (assessment-grid element). One Inhalt line for the writing acts of the text type, one for each bullet point naming what a full answer contains (taken from the Textbeilage), one for grasping the Textbeilage, correct facts and own thought; then Textstruktur (the conventions of the text type, the reference to the Textbeilage, cohesion), Stil und Ausdruck (suited to situation and readers, precise and varied, own formulations), Sprachnormen (spelling, punctuation, grammar), and the word range with what too long or too short would cost for this text type. Keep it to yourself until the learner has written; then give feedback along it as the assessment-grid element describes.
- **Do not** present a task you wrote as a real Matura task, do not reuse the tasks of this bundle with a new theme only, and vary the themes (school, media, environment, work, society, culture, technology, health, language).
