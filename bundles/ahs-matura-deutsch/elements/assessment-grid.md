# Introduction

- Try it: a Leserbrief has a brilliant style and no spelling mistakes, but it never says what the newspaper article it answers was about, although the task asked for that. Guess: can it still get a good grade?
- Matura texts are not graded by overall impression but with an official grid: four dimensions, 15 criteria and five levels. Knowing the grid tells you where a text gains and where it can fail.
- Afterwards the learner can sort any comment on a text into a dimension, knows how the grade follows and uses the grid on their own drafts.

# Core

Each of the two Matura texts is assessed in four dimensions: Inhalt (is
every instruction carried out with the writing acts of the named text
type, is the Textbeilage understood, are the facts right, how deep and
independent is the thinking), Textstruktur (is the text built and linked as
the text type requires, and is the Textbeilage worked in), Stil und
Ausdruck (does the language fit the writing act and the situation, are
words and sentences precise and varied, are the formulations the writer's
own) and Sprachnormen (spelling, punctuation, grammar). Each dimension
gets one of five levels, from "nicht erfüllt" to "weit über das
Wesentliche hinausgehend erfüllt". The levels are then combined into three
competence areas: Inhalt and Textstruktur of text 1 (K1), the same for text
2 (K2), and Stil and Sprachnormen of both texts (K3). In K1 and K2 every
dimension must reach at least "das Wesentliche überwiegend erfüllt"; in K3
a weak text can be balanced by a clearly positive other one. All three
areas must be positive for a positive grade, so strong language can never
make up for a task that was not carried out.

# Key facts

- Four dimensions with 15 criteria: Inhalt (5), Textstruktur (3), Stil und Ausdruck (4), Sprachnormen, also called normative Sprachrichtigkeit (3).
- Inhalt: (1) the writing acts (Schreibhandlungen) of the required text type are carried out; (2) the Arbeitsaufträge are fulfilled; (3) the Textbeilage is grasped as the instructions require; (4) the content is factually correct; (5) the quality of the thinking, on a scale from superficial and reproducing to complex, precise and independent.
- Textstruktur: (1) coherence: the text is built in thought and form as the text type requires, including its conventions (headline, introduction and conclusion, address and closing formula in a letter or speech); (2) reference to and linking with the Textbeilage as the text type requires (name the source, mark quotations and indirect speech); (3) suitable cohesive devices between sentences and paragraphs.
- Stil und Ausdruck: (1) language suited to the writing act and the situation; (2) appropriate and precise wording with varied vocabulary; (3) understandable, varied sentence structures that suit the text type; (4) own formulations instead of wording leaned on or copied from the Textbeilage.
- Sprachnormen: (1) spelling (das/dass counts here), (2) punctuation, (3) grammar. They are judged by the overall impression of how well the rules are applied, not by counting errors.
- Five levels: nicht erfüllt; das Wesentliche überwiegend erfüllt; das Wesentliche zur Gänze erfüllt; über das Wesentliche hinausgehend erfüllt; weit über das Wesentliche hinausgehend erfüllt. Only the four positive levels have descriptors; a dimension gets one level, weighed from the descriptors that apply.
- The levels follow the grade definitions of the LBVO: überwiegend = Genügend, zur Gänze = Befriedigend, über das Wesentliche = Gut, weit über das Wesentliche = Sehr gut.
- Three competence areas of equal weight: K1 (Inhalt and Textstruktur of text 1), K2 (the same of text 2), K3 (Stil and Sprachnormen, first per text as K3/1 and K3/2, then together). The teacher weighs the two dimensions of an area into one level when they differ.
- In K1 and K2 every dimension must be at least "überwiegend erfüllt", otherwise the whole area is "nicht erfüllt" and the exam is negative. In K3 one text may be negative if the other is clearly positive. All three areas positive = positive exam.
- Threshold for the year grades: they can only be taken into account when Inhalt is at least "überwiegend erfüllt" in at least one of the two texts.
- Automatically "nicht erfüllt": Inhalt when the topic is missed, the instructions are ignored, the central writing act is missing or the Textbeilage is not grasped even in its core; Textstruktur when no structure is recognisable or there is no reference to the Textbeilage at all; Stil when the text is hard to understand throughout or mostly copied from the Textbeilage.
- Text length: the word range is target ± 10 %. Missing it is not punished as such, but its effects are: a text that is too long misses the compression a Zusammenfassung, Leserbrief or Kommentar needs; one that is too short usually lacks the depth of a Textinterpretation, Textanalyse, Erörterung or Meinungsrede. In those longer types a somewhat longer text can show quality.

# Level: highschool

<!-- ptf:text v1 -->

Back to the Leserbrief with the brilliant style. The task asked for three
things, and one of them, saying what the article was about, is missing.
That is a gap in Inhalt (an instruction not fulfilled) and in Textstruktur
(no reference to the Textbeilage), and good style cannot fill it: Inhalt and
Textstruktur are judged for each text on its own, and each of them has to
reach at least "das Wesentliche überwiegend erfüllt".

The grid has four dimensions. Inhalt asks: did you do what each
instruction asks, in the right text type, did you understand the
Textbeilage, are your facts right, and how much of your own thinking is in
it? Textstruktur asks: is the text built as this text type is built, does
it work the Textbeilage in, and are sentences and paragraphs linked?
Stil und Ausdruck asks: does the language suit the readers and the
situation, are the words precise and the sentences varied, and are the
formulations your own? Sprachnormen covers spelling, punctuation and
grammar, judged as an overall impression.

Each dimension gets one of five levels. "Überwiegend" means Genügend, "zur
Gänze" Befriedigend, "über das Wesentliche hinaus" Gut and "weit über das
Wesentliche hinaus" Sehr gut. Then come the three areas: text 1, text 2,
and the language of both. The first two have no mercy: one dimension "nicht
erfüllt" and the area is lost. Language is more forgiving: if one text is
weak in style and errors but the other is clearly good, the area can still
be positive.

Use the grid on your own drafts: first check every bullet point and your
facts (Inhalt), then the build and the links to the Textbeilage
(Textstruktur), then read aloud for style and own wording, and last
proofread for your typical errors.

# Misconceptions

- **style-compensates**: A brilliant style makes up for a missing bullet point → Inhalt and Textstruktur of each text form their own area, and every dimension in it must be at least "überwiegend erfüllt"; style cannot replace a missing part of the task.
- **errors-only**: The grade is mainly the number of mistakes → Sprachnormen is one of four dimensions and is judged by overall impression, not by counting; two of the three areas are decided by Inhalt and Textstruktur alone.
- **average-grade**: The grade is the average of the dimensions → a positive grade needs all three areas positive; a single "nicht erfüllt" in Inhalt or Textstruktur makes the exam negative.
- **no-compensation**: Any weak dimension anywhere makes the exam negative → in the language area (K3) a negative text can be balanced by a clearly positive other text; only K1 and K2 allow no balancing.
- **copying-is-content**: Copying many sentences from the Textbeilage fills the Inhalt → copied wording fails the criterion of own formulations (Stil) and shows only reproducing thought (Inhalt); a text mostly copied is "nicht erfüllt" in Stil.
- **length-penalty**: Every word outside the range costs points → there is no deduction for length as such; what counts is what the length does to the text (too long loses compression, too short loses depth).

# Tutor notes

- When the learner hands in a text, give feedback in the order of the grid: Inhalt (each bullet point, Textbeilage, facts, depth), Textstruktur, Stil und Ausdruck, Sprachnormen, each with a level from the five and one concrete example from the text.
- Name a level per dimension by weighing the descriptors that apply; if some criteria are higher and some lower, decide by their number and weight, as the official instructions do.
- For a full theme package also give K1, K2 and K3 and say whether the exam would be positive. Remind the learner that in K1 and K2 nothing can be balanced.
- Do not mark every error: group errors by type (das/dass, commas, capitals) and name the two or three most frequent; let the learner find the instances. Judge Sprachnormen by overall impression, as the grid does.
- Watch for the automatic "nicht erfüllt" cases (topic missed, instruction ignored, Textbeilage not grasped, no reference to it, mostly copied wording) and name them first, because they decide the grade.
- Say which single change would raise the text most; usually it is in Inhalt.
- Be honest about the level, but do not predict an exam grade from one text.
- The official grid, the Handreichung with explanations of each criterion and the Korrektur- und Beurteilungsanleitung are published by the BMBWF on matura.gv.at; when the learner has them, use them alongside this element. This element paraphrases them and does not replace them.
